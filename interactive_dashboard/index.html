About this dashboard

When aircraft hit wildlife, which strikes cause damage?

Every year US pilots, airports and airlines report thousands of collisions between aircraft and birds, bats or ground animals. This dashboard explores of those reports from at US airports. Most strikes are harmless; the aim is to find the ones that are not.

Three questions

  1. How high, and in which phase? The Flight envelope: height above ground and flight phase.
  2. What hits the aircraft? Species groups, plus the animal-size and time-of-day chips.
  3. When and where? Strikes over time and the airport map.

How to explore

  • Every chart is a filter: drag across the timeline or height rows, click bars, phases, species or airports.
  • Filters combine across charts; each chart keeps showing what is outside its own selection.
  • Hover any mark for exact numbers. Reset clears everything.
  • Gray = strike counts, orange = damage, blue = your selection.

Read with care

  • Reporting is voluntary. Many fields were never recorded (for example height for of strikes); they are shown as "Not recorded", never guessed.
  • "% damaging" counts only strikes whose damage level was recorded, and is shown only when at least such strikes back it.
  • On the map, nearby small airports share one circle until you zoom in. strikes have no mappable airport and are not on the map.
  • The long rise in strikes is largely better reporting, not only more wildlife.

Data

  • FAA Wildlife Strike Database (wildlife.faa.gov), US airports only.
  • Airport locations: OurAirports (public domain).
  • State outlines: us-atlas (US Census Bureau).
